I researched two-player achievement games built on Ramsey Theory, where a win is defined as the first player to force a monochromatic \(k\)-clique on a graph of \(n\) nodes. Let \(D(n)\) denote the smallest clique size \(k\) at which every combination of strategies results in a draw. I measured the draw threshold \(D(n)\) using a Monte Carlo Python simulation across four edge-selection strategies: WIN (win on the next move), BLOCK (block the other player from winning on their next move), and RANDOM (select a random, unchosen edge). Through my simulations, I found that draw frequencies grow logistically with \(k\), and \(D(n)\) itself follows a stepwise, increasing pattern that is best approximated by a logarithmic function, \(D(n) \approx 2.362\ln(n) + 0.9905\). Additionally, I improved the Ramsey Games simulation algorithm itself. By **optimizing clique-detection and game-state evaluation to eliminate redundant computation**, my algorithm was able to nearly 8× the graph size we were able to simulate.
